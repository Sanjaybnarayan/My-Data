# ISO/IEC 27701 Privacy Information Management

**Not a compliance claim, and certification is not sought or held.** An
applicability review by a programmer. Legal review required. See
[MASTER_COMPLIANCE_MATRIX.md](MASTER_COMPLIANCE_MATRIX.md).

## Does it apply?

**Not to this application.** It extends 27001, and the same answer follows —
there is no management system and no organisation. See
[ISO_27001.md](ISO_27001.md).

Its vocabulary is still the clearest way to say what this application does with
data about people, so the useful controls are answered below.

| Control | Status | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Personal data identified and classified | `TESTED` | `js/data/classification.js` |
| Data minimisation | `DESIGNED` | `docs/DATA_GOVERNANCE.md` |
| Subject access | `IMPLEMENTED` | `js/reports/build.js` |

## Classification

Six levels, **derived from the schema rather than hand-labelled**. Five hundred
and eighteen fields labelled by hand would be five hundred and eighteen chances
to be wrong and one afternoon before the labels drifted. The level comes from
signals the schema already carries — field type, the `encrypted` flag, the
entity's access rules — so it cannot fall out of step.

`docs/DATA_CLASSIFICATION.md` records what that revealed, including the finding
that masking on classification alone would have made the application unusable,
because `person.name` classifies as highly sensitive and a family app that hides
everybody's name is not a family app.

## Minimisation — why only `DESIGNED`

**The schema is generous on purpose.** A household decides what to fill in, and
most fields stay empty. That is minimisation by *practice*, not by control:
nothing prevents a household recording more than it needs, and nothing prompts
it to record less.

`tools/field-coverage.mjs` is the closest thing — it keeps an inventory of every
field nothing reads, so a *new* one has to be a deliberate act. Seventy-five
fields are on that list and most are legitimately reference data. It is a
guard against dead fields rather than against over-collection, and the two are
easy to confuse.

## Subject access, and who the subject is

Export exists: CSV, XLSX and PDF writers, all dependency-free, over the
household's own records.

What does not exist is a **per-person** access path. There is no way for one
member to ask what is held about *them* specifically, and — the case that
matters — no route at all for a non-member. A household staff member cannot see
their own attendance record. That is recorded as a gap in [STAFF.md](STAFF.md)
rather than smoothed over here.
